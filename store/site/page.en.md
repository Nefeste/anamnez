Anamnesis is a doctor simulator with honest diagnostics. Every patient has a real illness or
injury, sometimes more than one, and almost every sign stays hidden until you ask, examine, or order
a test or an X-ray. The game is in Russian, and so are the screenshots.

## How to play

- **A shift at the clinic.** Patients come with and without appointments. The nurse takes
  temperature, pulse, blood pressure and oxygen saturation and sees urgent patients in first. The
  map shows who is where: at reception, in the waiting room, at the X-ray, in your office.
- **Examination.** A patient chart with tabs at the bottom, under your thumb. The history is a
  conversation: you ask, the patient tells. The physical exam is a tap on a body figure. Lab tests,
  X-rays, ECGs, ultrasound, CT, MRI and endoscopy go on a referral form — each with its own cost and
  time. While a test is running, you can send the patient to wait and see the next one; those who
  wait too long leave.
- **Hypotheses.** The patient chart holds a log of results and a hypothesis board: your own differential,
  and for each hypothesis what speaks for it, what speaks against it, what is still unchecked and what
  would confirm it.
- **Decision.** A diagnosis, a treatment sheet and where to treat: at home, on a ward or in the
  operating room — or, if needed, a referral to another hospital or an ambulance.
- **Review.** What it really was, a score for accuracy, justification, thrift and safety, and how
  an experienced doctor would have reasoned. Patients sent home sometimes get worse and come back,
  and the end-of-day summary tells you what happened to earlier patients.
- **Campaign.** A young doctor at a village clinic, and then at a district hospital with an
  emergency department, wards and an operating room, where a vascular unit with a CT room and an
  intensive care ward opens, and at a city hospital with a ventilator ICU, an infectious diseases
  unit, endoscopy and specialist doctors. The first shift is with a mentor; after that come letters
  and tasks without deadlines, and at the end, a look back at the whole career.
- **Your own hospital.** Offices, a lab, X-ray, ultrasound, CT, MRI and endoscopy rooms, an
  emergency department, wards, isolation boxes and an operating room, hiring, the cash desk. Doctors
  you hire see patients on their own, specialists have their own appointments, and new patients from
  the disease groups you pick are left for you. Insurance pays for a justified diagnosis and for
  tests that were indicated. The end-of-day summary shows where patients waited: for each place, how
  many went through, how long they waited and how long it was busy, with the bottleneck first.
- **Emergency department.** The ambulance brings in the seriously ill, and urgency is checked
  against the NEWS2 score. Seriously ill patients in the exam bay get first measures, from oxygen to
  a pressure dressing: the bay nurse gives them, and the bedside monitor shows what got worse. Without
  the measure they need, a seriously ill patient gets worse every 20–40 minutes, and a cardiac arrest
  calls a code blue: the team runs to the bed. You do a ward round every morning and discharge
  patients when they are better; a surgical team works in the operating room — and the longer an
  appendicitis or a perforated ulcer waits, the more dangerous it gets.
- **Four difficulty levels.** Trainee: “Looks like” hints, what relates to the complaint comes first
  in questions, examination and tests, and the review is gentler. Student: hints, and tests never
  err. Resident: up to three “Looks like” conditions with confidence in words, and tests sometimes err.
  Doctor: no hints, and tests sometimes err, as in real life.

## What’s inside

The game’s medical database holds over 120 illnesses, injuries and poisonings, and it grows with every
version. Patients come to you with over 110 conditions and injuries; the rest are poisonings and
chronic illnesses that run in the background of the main one.

### Clinic

- Conditions a general practitioner sees: from colds and flu to pneumonia, angina, heart attack,
  hypertensive crisis, pericarditis and acute heart failure.
- Rhythm problems: atrial fibrillation, tachycardias, heart block.
- Pulmonary embolism, hypoglycaemia and diabetic ketoacidosis, nosebleeds, hives and anaphylactic
  shock.

### Emergency department and surgery

- Surgical conditions: from biliary colic to a perforated ulcer, bowel obstruction, deep
  vein thrombosis and acute ischaemia of the leg.
- Injuries: fractures, knee ligament tears, a torn calf muscle, hand and scalp wounds, burns, chest
  trauma, concussion.
- Wounds and burns as in a real emergency department: stitch now or delay the stitches, check the
  finger tendons, measure a burn with the patient’s palms. Tetanus shots by vaccination records, and
  after a dog bite, a rabies vaccine with immunoglobulin.
- Operations — from appendectomy to hip replacement.

### Stroke unit

- Ischaemic stroke: thrombolysis within 4.5 hours, and the window closes while the work-up goes on.
- Brain haemorrhage, transient ischaemic attack, aortic dissection, pulmonary embolism — each with its
  own scores, its own scans and its own deadline.
- A drooping face is not always a stroke: it may be Bell’s palsy, and then no CT is needed.
- Seizures and status epilepticus: an anticonvulsant within 5 minutes.

### Infectious diseases ward

- Gut infections, meningococcal disease, herpes simplex encephalitis.
- After a tick bite — tick-borne encephalitis and Lyme disease.
- Botulism after home-canned food, viral hepatitis A and B, haemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome.
- An infectious patient never goes to a shared ward: there are isolation rooms for that.

### Images and tests

- The images are drawn by the program and differ for every patient: chest, abdominal and bone
  X-rays, abdominal, vein and artery ultrasound, 12-lead ECGs, head CT and MRI, spine MRI.
- Decision rules from clinical guidelines: whether an ankle, foot or knee X-ray is needed, whether a
  CT scan is needed after a blow to the head, whether a leg vein ultrasound is needed for leg pain.
- Signs don’t always show up and tests are sometimes wrong — as in real life. A diagnosis is a
  probability, and the review shows how sure an experienced doctor would have been.

### Encyclopedia, profile and modes

- “What is this?” for every sign and test in plain words, and more in the in-game encyclopedia. From
  a condition’s article you can work through a case — see one such patient.
- A doctor profile: specialties with levels and small skills, practice totals, an archive of visits
  with reviews, and achievements — no deadlines and no streaks.
- A “Shift” at a hospital of your choice and a “Case of the day” — one patient a day, the same for
  everyone; any of the last 30 days can be played at any time.
- Challenges — a shift with a condition: no history taking, no tests or scans, or no more than
  1,500 ₽ of tests per patient. Week-long scenarios — five shifts with a special patient flow and a
  goal of their own: “Winter flu”, “The hospital next door has closed”, “The X-ray is broken”.
- Two themes: the light “Chart” — paper and blue ink — and the dark “Monitor”. Or follow the phone.
- Progress moves to another phone as a file.

## Questions

### How much does Anamnesis cost?

It is free. There are no ads, purchases or subscriptions.

### Do I need the internet?

No. The game works offline and sends nothing anywhere — it doesn’t even have permission to access
the network. Saves stay on the phone; to move them to another phone, use a file — the transfer
screen in Settings.

### Is this a medical app? Can I treat myself with it?

No. Anamnesis is a game, not a medical device, and it does not replace a doctor. Diseases, tests and
treatment follow clinical guidelines but are simplified for the game, and the medical articles are
still drafts. If you or someone close to you feels unwell, see a doctor; in an emergency call 112
(103 is the ambulance in Russia).

### What age is it for?

16+. The game has serious illnesses, injuries, poisonings and patient deaths — not graphic, but not
played down either. If you’d rather not lose patients, turn on soft mode in Settings: instead of
dying, a patient is transferred to the regional hospital in a critical condition.

### Do I need medical training to play?

No. Every sign, test and disease is explained in plain words: tap “?” for a short note, and read
more in the in-game encyclopedia. The first campaign shift is with a mentor, and the easier
difficulty levels hint at what the illness looks like.

### How do the difficulty levels differ?

Trainee: hints, and what matters for the complaint comes first. Student: a “looks like”
hint with frequencies; tests and examination never err. Resident: up to three diseases in that hint,
confidence in words; tests sometimes err. Doctor: no hints; tests and examination sometimes err, as
in real life.

### What is the case of the day?

One patient a day, the same for every player. You can play any of the last 30 days; the first
attempt counts, and afterwards there is a review of what it really was.

### Can I build my own hospital?

Yes. In the campaign the hospital grows chapter by chapter, and after chapter 2 the sandbox opens:
your own hospital on an empty plot or from a ready clinic — offices, a lab, X-ray, ultrasound, CT,
wards, an operating room, hiring doctors and running the budget.

### How many chapters are there, and how does the campaign end?

Four chapters: from a village clinic to a city hospital with intensive care. Each has letters and
tasks without deadlines; after chapter 4 comes a look back at the whole career and a letter from the
mentor. You can run three careers, each at its own difficulty, and keep working in the hospital
after the finale.

### Found a bug or have an idea — where do I write?

The bug report in Settings gathers the version, phone and the day’s log, and you send it yourself.
Or write from the About screen. Email: support@gornitsa.games. We read RuStore reviews too.
